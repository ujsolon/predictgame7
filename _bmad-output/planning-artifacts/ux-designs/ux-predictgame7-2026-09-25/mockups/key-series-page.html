The series so far

Golden State took the first two games at Oracle Arena and added a 108–97 win in Game 4 to lead the Finals 3–1 — the position 31 previous Finals leaders all converted into a title. Cleveland has answered twice. A 112–97 win in Game 5 cut the deficit, and a 115–101 win in Game 6 in front of the Warriors' own crowd forced a winner-takes-all seventh game back at Oracle.

LeBron James has carried the series through six games — 31.2 points, 11.0 rebounds and 9.3 assists per night — with Kyrie Irving at 27.7 of his own. Stephen Curry has been held below his MVP average in all three Cleveland wins, and the Warriors' 40-point first half in Game 4 is now the high-water mark of their series. Game 7 arrives Sunday at Oracle. The models have their picks. The verdict hasn't been written.

How it ended

Game 7 stayed inside four points for its final six minutes. With 1:50 left and Cleveland up by one, LeBron James chased down Andre Iguodala for the block; 47 seconds later, Kyrie Irving hit a three over Stephen Curry to make it 92–89. The Cavaliers finished 93–89 — the lowest combined-scoring Game 7 of the decade.

James averaged 29.7 points, 11.3 rebounds and 8.9 assists across the seven games and recorded a triple-double (27–11–11) in the clincher. It remains the only 3–1 comeback in NBA Finals history — the 32nd team to face the deficit became the first to erase it.
